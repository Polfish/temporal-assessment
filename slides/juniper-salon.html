Filling the chair after a cancellation

Fewer empty chairs, and no more chasing people by text.

How it works today

  1. A cancellation shows up in Square.
  2. Lena or Carla look through the Google Sheet of people who asked for an earlier appointment.
  3. Several likely matches are texted at once from the salon phone, and whoever is watching the phone sorts out the replies.

What goes wrong

  • Nobody records who was texted or when, so follow-up is guesswork.
  • Two people say yes to the same chair, and untangling it depends on who is at the desk.
  • When nobody answers, the gap is noticed late or not at all, and the chair stays empty.
  • Time at the front desk goes to texting instead of clients.
“I’d be nervous about it offering the same opening to two people, or continuing after the opening is no longer available.” Lena, on what would make her distrust an automated version

The prototype

One opening, one orderly conversation at a time

1

Staff enter the opening

Service, stylist, and time. The screen shows who will be contacted and in what order before you start.

2

Clients are ranked

Same service only. A “required” stylist is respected. People who asked for this stylist go first, then whoever has waited longest.

3

One text at a time

The first person gets 15 minutes for a same-day opening (60 otherwise). A live countdown shows on the dashboard.

4

Yes, no, or silence

YES: confirmation text, chair held. NO or no reply: the next person is texted, and they stay on the waitlist.

5

Staff finish in Square

Book the accepted client as today. If they change their mind, one click withdraws them and resumes the search.

Why it cannot go wrong the way you feared

Four things the system guarantees

Never two offers at once

Each opening is a single process that holds exactly one offer at a time. A late YES from someone whose turn has passed is refused automatically, with a polite “sorry, already taken” text, and the person who holds the offer is unaffected.

Nobody has to watch the phone

Every offer has a countdown. When it runs out, the next person is texted without anyone noticing or remembering. The windows never run past the appointment time itself.

Stops the moment the opening is gone

“Cancel opening” ends the process instantly and tells whoever held the offer. If the appointment time arrives first, it stops by itself. Nothing is ever sent for a dead opening.

Nothing gets lost

Every text, reply, skip, and decision is recorded against the opening. The front desk always sees who holds the offer and who is next, and if a computer restarts the process carries on where it left off.

Underneath, the engine is Temporal: it keeps each opening’s state safe, runs the timers, delivers replies to the right process, and makes sure every step happens exactly once. Being honest about the prototype

What is simulated, and what is not built yet

Simulated
  • Text messages are not really sent. They appear in an on-screen “client phone” so we can act as the client. Connecting a real texting service is a small, contained change.
  • Square and the Google Sheet are replaced by a built-in sample waitlist of eight fictional clients. Staff enter openings by hand instead of Square announcing them.
  • Booking in Square stays manual, exactly as today. The system just tells you when to do it.
Not built yet
  • Stopping one client from holding offers for two different openings at the same time.
  • A “pause and handle this one by hand” mode. Today you can skip, record a reply yourself, or cancel.
  • Adding or editing waitlist clients inside the app.
  • Using each client’s general availability to filter, rather than just showing it to staff.
  • Reports after the fact: chairs filled, response times, no-shows.

A practical next step

A two-week pilot with Carla’s clients

Small enough to switch off in an afternoon, real enough to learn from.

The pilot
  1. Connect real texting on one salon number, for Carla’s waitlist only (about 20 people).
  2. Lena or Carla enter openings by hand when a cancellation lands; Square booking stays manual.
  3. Keep the 15-minute window for same-day openings and 60 minutes otherwise, and adjust after week one.
  4. Afterwards, connect Square so openings are created automatically.
What we measure
  • Chairs filled vs. left empty, compared with the two weeks before.
  • Minutes the front desk spends on cancellations each day.
  • Double-offer incidents. Target: zero.
  • How often clients reply within the window, to tune the timing.

What we need from you

  • Carla’s current waitlist, with “required” or “preferred” marked per client.
  • A yes on the wording of the four text messages.
